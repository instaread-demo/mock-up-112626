Israeli Passengers Face Bag Searches On Arrival At Amsterdam Airport After Dutch Ban On Goods Made in Illegal Settlements

by Mateusz Maszczynski
28th September 2026

Anyone traveling from Israel to Amsterdam faces the risk of having their bags searched on arrival for goods that were produced in illegal Israeli settlements in the West Bank after the Dutch government introduced new laws that apply retroactively.

Since September 2022, it has been illegal to bring in any goods that were manufactured or produced in Israeli settlements classified by the European Union as being unlawful. The EU maintains an extensive list of postcodes in Israel that were brought under Israeli control since 1967.

Dutch authorities have warned that the ban applies to all types of goods and it doesn’t matter whether it was bought as a souvenir, given as a gift, or is just for personal use.

It is up to the passenger to prove that the product they are bringing into the Netherlands did not come from an illegal settlement as classified by the EU. Many Israeli products now prominently list the postcode they were produced in to show compliance with these rules.

But anything that isn’t in its original packaging could be confiscated for falling foul of this law. Anything bought before the law came into effect is still covered and could be confiscated by customs authorities.

Some Israeli passengers who have flown with the national flag carrier El Al from Tel Aviv to Amsterdam Schiphol Airport say they have already seen customs searches target passengers from their flights, although it remains unclear whether these were just routine checks or specifically because of the new law.

Common items that could come under the Dutch ban include food and snack products, as well as Dead Sea cosmetics; however, the Israeli cosmetics company Ahava has long moved its production facilities out of the West Bank.

KLM Royal Dutch Airlines resumed services from Amsterdam to Tel Aviv on August 25, although the flight is not currently operating as a non-stop service. Instead, the plane makes a short stopover in Larnaca, Cyprus, where the crew swaps over with new crewmembers.

The first crew then spend the night in Larnaca to avoid a layover in Tel Aviv. The second crew flies from Larnaca to Tel Aviv, then returns immediately to Amsterdam on a non-stop service.
