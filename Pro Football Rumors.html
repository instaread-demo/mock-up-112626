Broncos, Riley Moss Agree To Extension

By Adam La Rose | at November 24, 2026 11:30 am

Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by

Instaread

NOVEMBER 24: Moss received a $6MM signing bonus, as detailed by Over the Cap. That represents part of his overall $35.63MM in guaranteed money on this new deal. Moss’ $14.12MM in 2027 compensation is fully locked in at signing, and his base salary for 2028 ($1.31MM) is set to vest on the fifth day of the new league year in March. The pact contains seven option bonuses to be paid out between 2027 and 2030.

NOVEMBER 19: Riley Moss will not reach free agency after this season. The fourth-year cornerback will instead continue his tenure with the Broncos.

Moss agreed to an extension on Thursday, as first reported by NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport. This is a four-year, $67MM deal, Tom Pelissero of The Ringer adds. Moss is set to remain in place with Denver through 2030 as a result of today’s news.

[RELATED: Broncos, Ja’Quan McMillian To Resume Talks In Offseason]

The Broncos extended Patrick Surtain in 2024 and gave him a raise in June. The top of the cornerback depth chart is not in doubt, although questions about Denver’s other, ascending, options at the CB spot have been raised based on the logjam at the position. Moss is now assured of a full-time starting role for the foreseeable future alongside Surtain.

Surtain’s pact carries an average annual value of $24MM. This Moss deal, meanwhile, checks in at $16.75MM in terms of AAV. Considering the cornerback market’s recent movement (producing six players averaging $30MM or more per season), this could prove to be a team-friendly deal as Moss plays out his prime.

Drafted in the third round in 2023, Moss played almost exclusively on special teams during his rookie season. Since then, he has been a defensive mainstay. Moss handled a 90% snap share during his second campaign and surpassed 1,000 snaps last year. A drop in overall usage has taken place so far in 2026, but the Iowa product has remained a starter through eight games.

Targeted on a regular basis by teams aiming to avoid Surtain, Moss led the NFL with 19 pass deflections in 2025. He has recorded eight so far this season while tallying an interception for the third year in a row. In coverage, Moss has allowed a 51.4% completion percentage as the nearest defender. That is the best mark of the 26-year-old’s career.

With Surtain and now Moss in the Broncos’ long-term plans, attention will turn to the future of slot starter Ja’Quan McMillian. He is also a pending free agent, and if anything today’s deal will no doubt lessen the chances of a second Broncos contract being worked out. 2025 first-rounder Jahdae Barron looms as a candidate to take on a first-team role in the event McMillian departs this spring. Kris Abrams-Draine – selected in the fifth round in 2024 – represents a depth option on the boundary.

Trade interest was shown in Denver’s non-Surtain corners, but general manager George Paton did not proceed with any swaps. It appeared as though no major developments would take place at the position after no extensions were signed leading up to Week 1. Moss is now entrenched as a core member of the Broncos’ defense with plenty of time still remaining in the 2026 campaign, however.

The Broncos currently sport a top-10 scoring defense despite ranking just 20th in passing yards allowed per game. Denver will look to improve in that regard down the stretch while competing for top spot in the AFC West and potentially the conference’s No. 1 seed. If the team’s secondary is to return to its previous form, Moss will have a large role now and well into the future.

    The Broncos have three really, really good starters in Surtain, Moss, and McMillian. We hear a lot about some corner being the best slot corner in the league from different places, but McMillian is a legitimate contender for that title. He tackles, breaks up passes, is athletic inside, and is in good position. He does the little things very well, and offers blitz value from the slot. Denver has been lucky to have him.

    The Barron pick is what causes some question. Obviously Denver wants to use him more to justify his selection, and he’s younger than the starting trio. I think Denver used Moss’ injury (and Surtain’s) to see how Barron and Abrams-Draine handled starting duties. In his snaps this year, Barron wasn’t bad, but there were times when he was out of position. I think there was a noticeable boost when Moss came back, just from my point of view watching.

    Barron wasn’t bad, understand, I just thought I saw a difference with Moss handling volume snaps in coverage. Moss plays with this wild style that makes you nervous to watch, but he’s effective…and many of the penalties that he gets look really iffy, to be honest. It’s strange to see so many head scratchers on a player who is admittedly very handsy. I think Denver was leaning towards letting Moss walk, but his absence and his play after returning convinced them otherwise.

    So, now, the question becomes this: do you let McMillian walk, or do you trade Barron? McMillian is really a great, underrated player, and you don’t know how Barron will handle being a slot corner full time. You also don’t want to relegate a first round pick to slot corner. But you have to go with your best lineup-and, if Moss’ athleticism falters, you would obviously put Barron outside. However, if you lose McMillian and go with Barron, you’re also taking youth (and the last years of a rookie deal) in the hopes that your first round pick can outplay your slot starter. There is the other issue of leadership, though. McMillian brings a lot of energy and example-setting to the defense, in addition to his playmaking. He gives the team a boost that will also have to be replicated. For that reason, and the return of having your first rounder as a slot corner, might make Denver decide to recoup some value in a trade, despite the youth.
