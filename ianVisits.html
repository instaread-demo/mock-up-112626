London reaches 2030 cycling target four years early

Published on 25th November 2026 by Ian Mansfield in Transport News

Listen to audio version of this article

Instaread

The number of daily cycle journeys in London rose to 1.66 million in 2026, taking the capital past its 1.6 million target four years ahead of its 2030 deadline.

Transport for London’s latest Travel in London report puts the annual increase at 10.1%, up from 1.5 million daily cycle rides in 2025. To put that into context, more people are now cycling each day than ride the trams.

Although naturally, it still falls far below other forms of public transport.

The Mayor of London has set a new target of 2.4 million daily cycle journeys by 2035. To support cycling, London’s Strategic Cycle Network reached 451km in September, with around 30% of Londoners living within 400 metres of the network. The Mayor aims to have 40% of Londoners living within 400 metres of a Cycleway by 2030.

Mayor of London Sadiq Khan attributed the rise in cycling to investment in routes and work by TfL, boroughs and campaigners, saying: “Having delivered a fivefold increase in Cycleways, record numbers of people are now choosing to cycle, with daily journeys increasing by nearly 60 per cent. Contrary to what the naysayers would have you believe, this growth shows that if you build high-quality cycling infrastructure people will use it, and the silent majority will be supportive.”

Although Tom Fyans, chief executive of the London Cycling Campaign, said the network would need to expand rapidly across the capital to meet the new 2035 target.
